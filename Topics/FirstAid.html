4 Point Plan:

  1. Primary survey (DRSABCD)
  2. Vital sign survey
  3. Secondary survey (Head to toe)
  4. Oxygen, Position, Reassurance

1. Primary Survey (DRSABCDE):

  • Danger: to self, others and patient. What might have caused their state?
  • Response COWS and AVPU
  • Send for help
  • Airway: clear and open airway, recovery position. To open airway: jaw thrust, head tilt
  • Breathing: check for 2 breaths in 10s. Look, listen, feel.
  • Circulation or CPR: check for pulse, control bleeding. For CPR: 30 compressions 2 breaths - use BVM
  • Defibrillation or Disability: apply defibrillator ASAP (if unconscious, not breathing), briefly interrupt CPR to allow defib to analyze rhythm
  • Environment or Exposure: protect from elements, consider HAZMAT

Response Assessment - COWS and AVPU:

  • Can you see me?
  • Open your eyes
  • What is your name?
  • Squeeze my hand
  • AVPU: Alert, Verbal, Pain responsive, Unresponsive

2. Vital Sign Survey, typical normal values:

  • Pulse: 60-100 bpm
  • Respirations: 12-20 breaths/min
  • Blood Pressure: 120/80 mmHg
  • Temperature: 36.5-37.2°C
  • Oxygen Saturation: 94-100%
  • Blood Glucose Level: 4-8 mmol/L
  • Pupil Size and Reactivity: 2-4 mm, PEARL (pupils equal and reactive to light)
  • Capillary Refill Time: < 2-3 seconds

3. Secondary Survey

Head to Toe Assessment including Inspection, Palpation, Auscultation.

  • Inspect: Look for any visible injuries, bleeding, deformities, abnormalities or pain.
  • Palpate: Feel for any tenderness, swelling, or unusual masses.
  • Auscultate: Listen to breath sounds, heart sounds, and bowel sounds.
  • Compare: Look for any differences between the left and right sides of the body.
  • Functional Assessment: Get the casualty to push and pull against your hands to check for strength, and to wiggle fingers and toes to check for sensation.
  • Check for any medical alert jewellery or tattoos.
  • Minimise neck movement if spinal injury is suspected.

Pain Assessment - OPQRST:

  • Onset/origin
  • Provoking factors
  • Quality: sharp, dull, throbbing
  • Region/radiating areas
  • Severity, 1-10
  • Time

Patient Handover - SAMPLE, ask if possible, or get information from bystanders:

  • Signs and Symptoms
  • Allergies
  • Medications
  • Past Medical History
  • Last Oral Intake - last ins and outs
  • Events Leading up to

4. Oxygen, Position, Reassurance:

Provide supplemental oxygen if available and indicated. Typically ~15 L/min

Position the patient accordingly to maximise breathing, circulation, comfort, injury management:

  • Unconscious and breathing: recovery position
  • Respiratory distress, chest pain: sitting up
  • Spinal injury: supine, immobilised
  • Shock: supine with legs elevated

Reassure the patient and keep them calm.
