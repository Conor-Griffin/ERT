Initial response

  • Conduct safe approach. Initial stand-off distance of minimum 70m, park uphill and upwind
  • Vital sign survey
  • Hazard assessment – chemical identification, fire? Spill? Gas leak? Need to contain? People?
    • Identify chemical, can use binoculars to see EIP (emergency information panels), or local knowledge, gas detectors. Can search ERG using name or UN number, or use ERG phone app
    • HAZMAT action guide (HAG) – sheet to fill out with chemical details, other details of incident such as appropriate PPE, firefighting, hazards
  • Secure the scene – may need evacuation, use cones, establish hot, warm, cold zones

Emergency information Panel and Hazchem code

  • Phone number is provided to get specialist advice
  • Containment vs dilution
    • Contain – prevent spillage entering drains or water
    • Dilute – substance may be washed with copious amounts of water
  • HAZCHEM code
    • Number indicates firefighting strategy
      1. Coarse spray (jets) – twist branch right
      2. Fine spray (fog)- twist branch left
      3. Foam
      4. Dry agent
    • First letter indicates PPE required and whether to dilute or contain spill
    • Second letter (if present) denotes Evacuation to be considered due to public safety hazard

First strike action

  • Establish hot, warm, cold zones
    • Hot zone: area around incident where contamination has occurred. Decontamination takes place between edge of hot and warm zones
    • Warm zone: outside the hot zone, back up crews on standby. PPE should be 1 level down from hot zone
    • Cold zone: area outside warm zone, for medical treatment, OIC and other support personnel, PPE is 1 level down from warm zone
  • HAZMAT PPE
    • LVL 1: LVL2 structural firefighting with respirator
    • LVL 2: Chemical splash suits with BA, wrists, ankles, hood taped up
    • LVL 3: Fully encapsulated Hazmat suit (with BA inside), wrists, ankles taped up
  • isolate/contain spill, communicate with CRO may be able to close valves remotely
  • Initiate action based on HAG/ERG/Hazchem code
  • Follow the principle of least exposure (least number of people, time, amount, and wear required PPE)
  • May be able to contain small spills using overdrum. Get barrel onto lid, then put overdrum over and screw lid down by twisting it.
  • Bring/organise someone to bring HAZMAT trailer to location
  • Utilise basket stretcher on wheels

Decontamination

  • Occurs in warm zone, should be bunded to contain chemical run off from suits.
  • Can have wet or dry decontamination. Dry should be used if the chemical reacts violently/unfavourably with water
    • Wet involves washing of person with water, dry involves removal of contaminants by brushing or vacuum
    • Using firehose to wet decon, use a gentle spray to ensure all water is contained in bund. Wet decon should occur head to toe, they turn around, one foot decon’d at a time and placed in warm zone. The person with less air in the team of 2 should be decon’d first (after casualty)
  • Partial decontamination of casualty/rescuer can also be performed in a safety shower prior if there is one within the hot zone.

Hazard classes

  • Class 1: Explosives
  • Class 2: Gases
  • Class 3: Flammable liquids
  • Class 4: Flammable solids
  • Class 5: Oxidising substances and organic peroxides
  • Class 6: Toxic and infectious substances
  • Class 7: Radioactive material
  • Class 8: Corrosive substances
  • Class 9: Miscellaneous dangerous goods
